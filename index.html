For coaches and therapists

Coach: “Let’s spend the last few minutes of this session building your next Focus File.”

A plan of action, instead of a “should.”

The client builds it—their motivation, their habits, their goals, in their own words. When a check-in catches a drift, those words stare back—not an assignment. Each block records what was finished, what helped, what pulled them off, where they stopped. A few blocks in, that’s the pattern: the conditions where their focus actually works.

ADHD coaching

External structure for starting, check-ins against time blindness, and a record that’s kinder than the client’s memory of the week.

Stuck patterns

The client names their trap during setup; the file quotes it back at the exact moment it’s happening.

Extra accountability

The file does the midweek checking, in the client’s own words—so the accountability doesn’t have to come from you.

Coach, next session: “How did your week go?”

The whole story, instead of “it went okay.”

AI asks the questions and insists on specifics; the client gives the answers; the file just holds them—it gathers and counts, never interprets. Digging beneath the pattern and building the new strategy is your craft—and the next Focus File is where it lands.

The client holds the record. Everything stays on their device—no account, no server, nothing sent to us. You see only what they choose to share.
