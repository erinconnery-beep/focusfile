The Ranch

One of the last great land empires of the American West.

Established in 1905 and held for generations, Climbing Arrow spreads across the north end of Montana's Bridger Range in five contiguous units — a working cattle kingdom on a scale that no longer comes to market.

  • 79,582± total acres, roughly 73,180 of them deeded, unfolding across four Montana counties.
  • A commercial Black Angus operation running close to 2,000 head, with legendary three-day cattle drives each June.
  • End-to-end privacy — mountain pastures, hay meadows, timbered ridges and open range, all under one brand.
  • Less than an hour from Bozeman and the Gallatin Valley, yet a world removed from it.

The Land & The Map

Terrain that runs from valley hay ground to alpine sky.

Five units stitch together the full vertical range of Montana — irrigated bottoms, rolling foothills of the Horseshoe Hills, and the timbered high country of the northern Bridgers.

  • Bridger Mountain Range frontage anchoring the ranch's eastern high country.
  • Irrigated hay meadows along the valley floor feeding the cattle operation year-round.
  • Miles of Sixteenmile Creek drainage — North Fork, Middle Fork and main channel — threading the property.
  • Contiguous, deeded scale that makes the ranch its own private landscape.

Hunting

Unsurpassed bull-elk hunting, held private for generations.

The Climbing Arrow's mix of timber, water and remote high pasture has made it one of Montana's most storied elk grounds — quiet country where the bugle still carries for miles.

  • Trophy bull elk moving between the timbered ridges and the creek bottoms.
  • Mule deer, whitetail and upland birds across the varied terrain.
  • Vast unpressured acreage — the kind of privacy that grows big animals.
  • A genuine self-contained wildlife habitat, managed and undisturbed.

Fishing

Twenty-plus miles of blue-ribbon trout water.

The North Fork, Middle Fork and main channel of Sixteenmile Creek run wild through the ranch — spring-fed, cold and private, holding native cutthroat, rainbows and wild German browns.

  • 20+ miles of private, walk-and-wade trout water.
  • Wild browns, cutthroat and rainbows in cold, clear current.
  • Spring-fed Sixteenmile Creek forks braiding the valley floor.
  • Fishing so private it feels like it was made for one rod.
